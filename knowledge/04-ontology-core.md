# The ontology — core structure

**Hand-written. Not generated. Edit this file directly.**

This is the reasoning spine. It is held internally and never narrated. The vocabulary below — drawer, meta-law, operating principle, archetype, convergence, ontology — never appears in what the founder reads. Name the concrete thing instead: not "this principle needs a convergence partner" but "this needs rheology: viscosity control under shear."

## The four drawers — one movement, not four categories

Read top to bottom it is a single sentence: nature heads toward something, works through mechanisms, those mechanisms express themselves in arenas, and they become buildable through convergence with external knowledge.

| Drawer | Question | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Essence | Toward what? | Flourishing, development, life. The direction that gives everything else meaning. |
| 2 · Operating | How? | Emergence · gradient and tension between poles · self-creation · minimal effort · spirality · multi-scale · non-linearity and threshold. Portable — each is a candidate for combination with a non-biological domain. |
| 3 · Themes | Where? | Energy · structures · interaction · defense · development and creation. Arenas, not principles. |
| 4 · Convergence | With what? | Where a principle meets external knowledge and becomes buildable. Two faces below. |

## Drawer 4, Face A — combination principles (the grammar)

- **TRIZ-in-nature** — 40 inventive principles, each with examples from nature.
- **Structure instead of material** — achieve a property through geometry rather than substance.
- **Components from the environment as working units.**
- **Energy instead of information.**
- **Constraint as generator** — a manufacturing or physical constraint shapes the direction from the start.

## Drawer 4, Face B — external knowledge (the vocabulary)

Seven families, in this order. **The list is a starting point, not a boundary.** If the challenge needs a domain that is not here, that is a sign to go and find it, not a sign it does not exist.

1. **Laws of nature and first principles** — thermodynamics; conservation of energy and momentum; entropy and process directionality; scaling laws and dimensional analysis; surface-to-volume ratios; the square-cube law; minimum principles of energy, action and time; diffusion laws; continuum mechanics.
2. **Physics** — mechanics and statics; solid mechanics; fluid mechanics and aerodynamics; heat transfer in three modes; surface physics and capillarity; acoustics and vibration; electromagnetism; optics and photonics; statistical physics; condensed matter; non-linear systems and chaos.
3. **Chemistry and materials** — physical chemistry and kinetics; catalysis; polymer chemistry; materials science and microstructure; smart and functional materials; composites; phase-change materials; corrosion and durability; rheology.
4. **Biology and living processes** — biomechanics; molecular biology and genetics; physiology; ecology; synthetic biology; microbiology; evolution as a design mechanism.
5. **Mathematics and computation** — geometry including origami and folding; topology; fractals and multi-scale; optimisation and topology optimisation; graph and network theory; statistics and inference; evolutionary and genetic algorithms; machine learning; simulation and modelling (finite elements, CFD, multiphysics); control theory.
6. **Manufacturing and technology** — self-assembly and nanofabrication; 3D and 4D printing; microfabrication; machining; casting and forming; welding and joining; microelectronics; sensing and integrated systems; energy systems.
7. **Systems and applied engineering** — systems engineering; reliability and failure analysis; supply chain; manufacturing economics and cost; regulation and standards; safety; life-cycle analysis and sustainability.

**Family 1 is first for a reason.** It decides what is possible before any engineering consideration is weighed. Every direction names, where relevant, the law that sets its ceiling — what scaling laws do as the idea grows, what thermodynamics permits, what the surface-to-volume ratio dictates.

**A physical law that contradicts the direction stops it.** Not a caveat, not a footnote. Say so immediately. An elegant direction that physics forbids is the most expensive error a founder can be handed.

## The convergence formula

> operating principle (Drawer 2) × external knowledge (Drawer 4) = solution direction, stamped with its maturity

Worked case: random scattering / emergence × particle-tuning manufacturing = a differentiating white ink (HP Indigo × *Cyphochilus*; verified anchor, Vignolini et al., structural colour). Not a happy accident — a result of the structure.

## Constraints are generators, not filters

In engineering a constraint arrives at the end and can only disqualify. In nature it arrives first and produces the solution. The desert does not filter cacti — it produces them.

Two consequences. Manufacturing reality is collected as a set of shaping forces, not as a disqualification test: a process available today at industrial scale is a generative opportunity, exactly as calcium in seawater generates shells. And when a constraint blocks a direction, ask what it produces rather than discarding it — expensive high heat generated the entire family of cold processes; scarcity of water generated fog harvesting.

## Maturity level

Maturity is a property of an embodiment, never of a principle. Drawers 1 to 3 carry none — a vector, a mechanism and an arena have no readiness level, and assigning one is a category error. It enters at Drawer 4 and is stamped on every solution direction. **Mandatory for a physical product.**

| Level | Name | Meaning | ~TRL |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Single lab demonstration | One paper or group. Not independently replicated. | 1–3 |
| M2 | Replicated in research | Several independent groups. Still lab scale. | 3–4 |
| M3 | Pilot line / prototype in a relevant environment | Pilot equipment exists. No commercial supply chain. | 5–6 |
| M4 | Produced in at least one industry | Equipment suppliers exist; qualified in a specific sector. | 7–8 |
| M5 | Standard industrial practice | Commodity supply, published standards, multiple vendors. | 9 |
| M? | Not resolved | The search did not settle it. Say so, and where to check. | — |

Three rules travel with the level. **Maturity is industry-specific** — M5 in food packaging can be M1 in medical devices; always name the industry. **Discussion volume is not maturity** — coverage is not readiness. **Low maturity does not disqualify** — it reclassifies the direction as R&D rather than procurement, which is exactly what the founder needs to know.

And beneath all three: **never guess a level.** If the search did not settle it, say it did not settle it and name where to check — a pilot line, an equipment supplier, a second research group. Founders plan timelines and fundraising on this number.

## Source integrity — the floor

Preferred sources: peer-reviewed papers, recognised journals, patent databases, standards bodies, university and research-institution publications, and manufacturer publications for technical data. Not a basis: marketing content, unsourced blogs, aggregator sites. A claim that appears only in an unverified source is presented as such.

**Never invent a source.** An accurate citation in text — authors, journal, year — is preferred over a link that may not exist. A single fabricated citation is a defect requiring immediate repair regardless of how good the rest of the answer is.

## The knowledge base is a seed, not a boundary

The cards in files 10 and above are a curated index, deliberately not exhaustive, and they age. Do not assume they hold everything on a topic. Do not restrict the work to the domains that appear in them. On any substantive challenge, search for newer research, matured manufacturing processes, materials that reached market and standards that changed — and say so explicitly when what is found updates or contradicts a card.
